Three Pillars

Three Pillars

Land

Stewardship First

Soil health, rotational grazing, and River Friendly certification aren't marketing — they're the operating system. The land improves every year, which means the product does too.

People

Community as Infrastructure

Farmers market regulars, student interns, church networks, and delivery customers form a moat no competitor can buy. Relationships compound faster than ad spend.

Purpose

Faith-Driven Consistency

The biblical foundation gives the brand a spine. In a market full of wellness claims, Agape offers something deeper — and that depth is what people remember and share.

Months 1–12

Foundation

  • SEO and local visibility (Clarksville pilot) — own the home turf first
  • Thanksgiving delivery expansion — prove the 200-mile model with one high-stakes season
  • Launch email list and basic CRM to capture every customer relationship
  • Document the farm: photo library, short videos, founder story on video
  • Formalize the River Friendly and regenerative certifications in all marketing

Year one is about being findable and memorable.

Months 13–24

Channels

  • Expand delivery to year-round: eggs, seasonal meat boxes, holiday specials
  • Wholesale conversations with Nashville restaurants and natural grocers
  • Launch a simple subscription model for eggs and monthly meat shares
  • Begin content cadence: monthly farm updates, seasonal guides, behind-the-scenes
  • Explore agritourism: farm visits, school field trips tied to the internship program

Year two turns one-time buyers into recurring relationships.

Months 25–36

Scale

  • Evaluate second processing or butchering partnership to remove bottlenecks
  • Hire or contract a part-time brand/marketing role
  • Expand the internship program into a named pipeline for future farm labor
  • Test adjacent products: value-added items, apparel, or branded goods
  • Measure everything: customer lifetime value, repeat rate, cost per acquisition by channel

Year three is when the brand stops being a farm with a side hustle.

Months 37–60

Legacy

  • Regional recognition: the regenerative farm of Middle Tennessee as a default search result
  • Potential second location or satellite market presence in Nashville
  • Formalize succession and land-trust or easement planning if multi-generational
  • Publish the farm's regenerative data — soil tests, carbon impact — as open proof
  • The brand outlives any single product line because it's built on land and trust

By year five, Agape isn't competing on price. It's the name people trust.
